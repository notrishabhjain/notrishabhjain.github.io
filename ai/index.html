How I think about it

Six habits for putting AI into a workflow

01

Start with the workflow, not the model

The first question is which decision, task or bottleneck we are trying to improve. AI is worth it when it changes the economics, speed, quality or accessibility of a real workflow.

02

Define the AI boundary

In regulated work, AI does not inherit authority. Decide what it may recommend, what it may automate, what needs a person's approval, what evidence must come with an output, and what happens when confidence is low.

03

Build the evaluation before declaring success

Acceptance cannot rest on a generic benchmark. Use golden datasets, cases already reviewed by people, human-versus-machine comparison, exception analysis and domain-expert review.

04

Introduce autonomy progressively

Shadow, then limited authority, then an expanded window, then production. Each expansion rests on evidence from the stage before.

05

Make outputs traceable

In government and regulated systems an answer without provenance is not enough. Data, model, evidence, validation, human decision, auditable outcome.

06

Measure the system, not just the model

The metric might be processing time, MTTR, SLA, false positives, human acceptance, cost, adoption or auditability. The model is one component of the product.

Where AI stays out

Holding it back is a product decision

GHCI launched without AI in Phase 1. The auditors had to understand and operate the process before they could judge an assistant working on top of it. That cost a phase of efficiency, and it means Phase 2 will be accepted on evidence.
