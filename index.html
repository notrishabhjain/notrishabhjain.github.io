Applied AI

AI earns its authority in stages

In regulated work an AI answer needs a boundary, evidence and an owner. These are the three habits I bring to every programme.

Define the boundary

What may it decide, and who answers?

On GHCI the rule is simple: AI advises, and a named officer approves every consequential action. Every generated claim cites its source document.

Evaluate before you scale

Measured against people

Translation in 22 languages, scoring at ±10% of human auditors, a golden dataset of cases already answered by hand. Quality is a number someone manages.

Autonomy by evidence

Shadow, window, production

On the wind portfolio AI moved from shadow mode to a six-hour window to full-day operation, one stage at a time, and was never rolled back.

What links the work

The hard part of transformation is rarely the software.

A statute may need to change. A procurement model may need to change. An organisation may need to adopt a new workflow. An AI system may need a boundary that a person owns. An architecture may need to favour resilience over analytical elegance.

I work where those things meet, and I design them together.
