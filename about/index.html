About

Engineer, consultant, now accountable for delivery

AI & Technical Project Manager at BDO India, delivering national platforms for central government. Based in Delhi, open to relocation and to remote work.

I spent six years writing production software, from mainframe automation for a UK bank to enterprise blockchain. At EY I moved into government consulting: authoring DPRs and RFPs for national platforms, then sitting on the ministry’s side of the table to hold vendors to them. Since 2024 I have been accountable for whole programmes, first a $2M+ enterprise portfolio at Infosys and now national platforms at BDO India.

The programmes I am proudest of share a pattern: a hard constraint, and several organisations that do not report to each other. A case platform that had to go live on a date fixed in law. A certification scheme with no process to copy. A national registry that needed its statute amended before any software could matter. On each I owned the delivery end to end: scope, schedule, budget, people, vendors and governance.

AI is now central to how I work and what I deliver. Five AI initiatives sit across my programmes, three of them live. Each was measured against people before anyone relied on it, and in government work a named officer stays accountable for every decision.

Large programmes succeed when the product, the process, the rules and the technology are designed together, and one person is accountable for all four.

How I work

A delivery playbook, built on real programmes

Six principles I apply on every engagement, each with the evidence from my own work.

01

Anchor the plan on what cannot move

A statute, a date fixed in law, an audit window, a budget. I find the immovable constraint in discovery and build the critical path around it, not the other way round.

EvidenceRegistry: the statute was amended before the build. Commission platform: the plan cut from five sprints to two to hold a legal date.

02

Baseline the scope, then control every change

Requirements are signed before the first sprint. After that, every change is a written, costed request with an impact analysis and a client decision.

EvidenceCertification platform: about 250 features baselined before sprint one; the first post-launch change delivered in about three days.

03

Govern what you do not control

A weekly steering meeting where decisions are taken, one status report with an owner for every dependency, and escalation paths agreed before they are needed.

EvidenceCommission platform: four organisations, no reporting line between them, live on the date.

04

Run quality and security as delivery streams

Internal reviews before external audits, phased user testing with real users, and a code freeze while findings are fixed. Nothing waits for a gate at the end.

EvidenceOver 90% of internal findings closed before the external audit; both national platforms cleared before launch.

05

Put AI in only where it can be measured

Agree the acceptance test before the build, compare the output against people, roll out in stages that can be reversed, and keep a named person accountable.

EvidenceWebsite scoring within ±10% of human auditors; support-ticket AI in production and never rolled back.

06

Measure outcomes, not deliverables

Profit, uptime, processing time and adoption. “Delivered on time” is the minimum, not the result.

EvidenceA portfolio from a loss to about 15% profit in six months; 99.9% service levels; registry processing more than 70% faster.
