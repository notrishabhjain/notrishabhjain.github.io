About

I make large, messy programmes land.

I started as an engineer — automating mainframe processes for a UK bank, then building blockchain systems that reached production. That gave me something many project managers don’t have: I can read the architecture, question an estimate and talk to engineers in their language.

At EY I moved into government consulting, working with senior officials on national platforms. I learned that the hardest blockers are rarely technical. On one programme, the blocker was an Act of Parliament — so we changed it.

For the last few years I’ve run whole programmes end to end: a $2M+/yr enterprise portfolio for a global wind-energy company, and now two national platforms for India’s e-governance division — one launched by a Minister, one built in about a month against a deadline fixed by law.

I’m now looking to bring that to international teams — as a full-time remote programme lead or on focused contract engagements.

Principles

What you can expect working with me

Constraints first

I find what can’t move — a law, a date, an audit, a budget — and plan around it before anything else.

Trade-offs in writing

Every decision has a cost. I name it, so sponsors decide knowing both sides, and nobody is surprised later.

Alignment without authority

Ministries, vendors, cloud teams and auditors rarely report to the PM. Written decisions and named owners keep them moving together.

Outcomes over outputs

Processing time, uptime, margin and adoption — not “delivered on time” — are how I judge a programme.
