Case study 02 · MNRE — National Green Hydrogen Mission · Project Manager, Delivery & UX Lead · 2026

Green Hydrogen Certification of India

A national certification registry for a process that did not previously exist, built append-only, passed through a CERT-In empanelled audit in a single pass, with a ₹2 crore AI layer scoped to stay advisory.

  • No existing process
  • Audit before the audit
  • AI after Phase 1
  • Live from June

01 — Context

A certification process that did not yet exist

The Green Hydrogen Certification of India scheme sits under the National Green Hydrogen Mission at MNRE. India did not have an established digital certification process for green hydrogen of this nature, so there was no mature as-is process to digitise.

The engagement therefore required process definition, product design, technology delivery, stakeholder coordination, security certification and future AI design to happen together, not in sequence.

02 — Challenge

Define the process, build the registry, and pass the audit — concurrently

  • No pre-existing certification process, and therefore no benchmark to build against
  • Four certificate types, each needing an auditable record of every amendment
  • A CERT-In empanelled security audit standing between the build and production
  • Validation rules that change through office memoranda rather than through releases
  • Auditors who had never operated the digital process they were about to be measured on

03 — Mandate

Lead project delivery and UX while translating the emerging certification process into a production-ready national digital registry — and author the Phase 2 AI scope.

04 — Critical decisions

Decision 01

Design the registry around auditability.Why. The registry is append-only. An amendment becomes a new record while the original remains intact, preserving the trail across all four certificate types. Certification is a legal artefact; overwriting one destroys the evidence of how it was reached.

Decision 02

Bring security into delivery rather than waiting for the audit.Why. Instead of letting the external audit be the moment issues surface, the programme ran internal pre-auditing with remediation in parallel.

Decision 03

AI was deliberately excluded from Phase 1.Why. Auditors first needed to understand and operate the underlying process. AI assistance was introduced only once users could meaningfully evaluate it — because an assistant nobody can judge is an assistant nobody should trust.

05 — Security outcome

48 / 51findings closed in internal pre-audit
Single passCERT-In empanelled audit
3findings carried, manually verified
CTOsigned the residual risk

Forty-eight of fifty-one findings were closed during internal pre-audit before the CERT-In empanelled audit opened, and the platform subsequently passed in a single pass. The three remaining findings came from customised reusable components, were manually verified, and the residual risk was signed by the CTO rather than quietly carried.

06 — Applied AI

₹2 crore of AI scope, written to be governable

I authored 80–90% of the Phase 2 AI scope — approximately ₹2 crore over about six months, spanning data scientists through testers.

  • Submission-gap analysis — what is missing from a producer's filing
  • Validation against rules that change through office memoranda, not through releases
  • Auditor checklist pre-fill, so review starts from a draft rather than a blank form
  • Emissions reconciliation
  • Evidence-backed assistance throughout

Two guardrails that are not negotiable

AI advises. A named officer approves every consequential action.Why. An incorrect certification decision carries financial and legal consequences. Authority therefore stays with a person who can be held to it.
Every generated claim cites the document supporting it.Why. In a regulated workflow, an answer without provenance is not an answer. The auditor must be able to go to the source.
  • Data
  • AI / model
  • Evidence
  • Validation
  • Named officer
  • Auditable certificate

07 — Evaluation

The benchmark had to be built before the AI could be judged

Acceptance is based on a golden dataset: cases previously answered manually become the reference against which AI performance is measured. Because the certification process itself was new, the evaluation methodology had to be designed as part of the programme — there was no pre-existing benchmark to borrow.

08 — Outcome

A national green hydrogen certification registry went live in June, with producers registering on the same public launch date. Phase 2 AI scope is authored and funded at approximately ₹2 crore.

09 — What this demonstrates

Responsible AI in a regulated workflow: defining a certification process that did not previously exist, building an append-only registry around it, passing a CERT-In empanelled audit in a single pass, and scoping an AI layer whose authority, evidence requirements and evaluation method were decided before a model was chosen.
