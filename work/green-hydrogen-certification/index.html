Context

The situation

To count as green, hydrogen must be made from renewable power and stay under a set carbon limit — and someone has to verify and certify that. India’s scheme rules were issued in April 2025, backing a national target of 5 million tonnes a year by 2030.

What didn’t exist was any way to apply, audit, approve and issue certificates online. There was no previous process to copy, the launch date was fixed, the cloud environment arrived only two and a half weeks before the first testing target, and an external security audit stood between the build and go-live.

Key decisions

The calls I made, and what each one cost

Define the process before building it

We started from the scheme rules, mapped every user journey, studied comparable portals and walked the client through the flow. Nothing went to development until it was accepted; ~250 features across 11 modules were baselined, and every later change went through formal change control.

Trade-off acceptedA slower start on code, in exchange for a scope that didn’t move under us.

Never overwrite a certificate

Every amendment creates a new version and the original stays readable, across all four certificate types — so the full history can be shown years later if a certification is challenged.

Trade-off acceptedMore storage and more complex screens that must always say which version you’re looking at.

Test security before the auditors arrive

We reviewed our own code first and closed 48 of 51 findings before the external audit began. The audit then needed one short round of fixes; clearance came on 30 June, the day before launch.

Trade-off acceptedTwo weeks of build time spent on remediation instead of features.

Launch without AI, add it in Phase 2

Verifiers and approvers had never used a digital process for this. They needed to understand it first, so AI was held back and scoped as an approved Phase 2.

Trade-off acceptedA less impressive launch, in exchange for a Phase 2 that can be accepted on evidence.

Results

What changed

  • Launched by the Minister on 1 July 2026, on the planned date.
  • 8 producers, 3 verification agencies and 4 committee members working on one platform; 20+ applications and 8 certificates issued so far.
  • National e-sign and digital-certificate signing, notifications and a document locker all live.
  • The first post-launch change request touched the workflow itself — analysed, approved and ready in about three days.
Where AI fits

AI with a named owner

Phase 2 — approved and in procurement — adds AI to check submissions, flag unusual emissions data and support verifiers. Two rules: AI advises and a named officer approves, and every AI claim must point to the source document. It will be accepted against cases people have already decided by hand.

Takeaway

What I carry forward

When a process is new, the people who judge the result have to understand it before you automate it. Time spent agreeing the process before development is what makes a fixed launch date possible.
