Case study 01 · Government of India · Project Manager · 2026

8th CPC Demand Management System

A national memorandum and demand-processing platform delivered against a fixed statutory tenure — and the AI examination layer now being built on it.

  • Statutory deadline
  • Parallelise delivery
  • 8 modules, ~4 weeks
  • Live from August

01 — Context

A statutory commission, a spreadsheet, and a date that could not move

The 8th Central Pay Commission processes memoranda and demands submitted by staff associations, ministries and federations. When the engagement began the process was spreadsheet-driven, and the Commission operates against a fixed statutory tenure.

The platform had to serve 10 processing officers and some 100 ministry users, absorb a migration across 14 offices with no common structure, and go live under coordination across four bodies that have no direct reporting relationship to one another.

02 — Challenge

The deadline was the one thing that could not be negotiated

On most programmes, a date moves when requirements, dependencies or coordination become difficult. Here it could not. The statutory tenure fixed the delivery date, so every other variable had to absorb the risk instead.

  • A statutory tenure that fixed the delivery date
  • A spreadsheet-driven process to be replaced by a governed workflow
  • Volumes rising by an order of magnitude within months of launch
  • Four coordinating bodies with no direct reporting relationship
  • Migration across 14 offices with no shared data structure
  • Government of India requirements for open-source technology and government-cloud hosting

03 — Mandate

Lead project delivery, product and workflow definition, and stakeholder coordination — and lead the transition from the initial operational platform toward an AI-assisted examination layer.

04 — Critical decisions

Decision 01

Parallelise the delivery rather than sequence it.Why. Eight modules were structured as parallel streams across two sprints, so schedule risk could be managed through coordination rather than left exposed at the statutory deadline.

Decision 02

Turn the spreadsheet into a governed workflow.Why. A state-driven demand lifecycle with role-based access replaced an unaccountable shared file. Every demand now has a state, an owner and an audit trail.
  • Creation
  • Examination
  • Ministry reply
  • Peer referral
  • Reviewing officer
  • Commission placement

Decision 03

Design for the next six months, not just launch day.Why. The system went live in August with roughly 1,500 memoranda and 50,000 demands, and was built for the 18,000 memoranda and some 200,000 demands expected within six months as the next tranche of data arrives.

05 — Applied AI

From page-by-page reading to evidence-assisted examination

I authored the AI scope now in build. The product objective is not to automate the examination — it is to stop officers reading every page to find the one that matters.

  • Anomaly detection across submitted demands
  • Duplicate detection, so the same demand arriving through several associations is recognised as one
  • Evidence matching against the memoranda a demand cites
  • Retrieval of referenced sources so the officer sees the basis, not just the claim

The division of labour is deliberate: Power BI provides analytical visualisation; AI provides the examination-support layer. Neither is asked to do the other's job, and neither makes the decision.

06 — Delivery complexity

Four bodies, no reporting line between them

Migration ran across 14 offices with no common structure, and go-live had to be coordinated across four bodies without a direct reporting relationship — which means the only available levers are institutional, not managerial.

When vendor resolution stalled, escalation was taken through the appropriate institutional channel while the underlying SSL and routing fault was diagnosed from the technology side in parallel. Waiting on one track while the other was available was not an option against a statutory date.

07 — Outcome

8modules delivered
~4weeks, mandate to go-live
7people on the delivery team
Auglive operation from

Eight modules delivered in approximately four weeks with seven people, followed by live operation from August. The platform is designed for substantially larger demand volumes while remaining aligned to Government of India requirements for open-source technology and government-cloud hosting.

08 — What this demonstrates

Delivery under extreme institutional and operational constraints: taking a nationally significant workflow from a spreadsheet through product definition, parallelised delivery, multi-agency go-live, and into an AI-assisted examination layer — against a deadline set by statute rather than by a plan.
