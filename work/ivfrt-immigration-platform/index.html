01 — Situation

Four services, separate databases, long-established local practice

The Immigration, Visa and Foreigners Registration & Tracking programme covers visa, e-visa, OCI and NRI services. Version 2.0 consolidated them onto one platform through staged national rollout.

The environment was operationally fragile: separate databases, application silos, and long-established local processes that differed from port to port.

02 — The constraint

Ports cannot stop while you improve the architecture

Immigration processing at a port of entry has no maintenance window and no graceful degradation. The consolidation that made architectural sense — collapsing the siloed databases into one analysable store — was also the change most likely to destabilise the systems officers depend on in real time.

Written specifications were also an unreliable guide: documented process and actual practice at a port had diverged over years.

03 — My mandate

Lead UI/UX and process re-engineering with business analysts, developers and designers.

04 — Decisions

Decision 01

Design from observation of officers and travellers, not from the written specification.

WhyThe specification described the process as designed; the ports ran the process as evolved. I worked from direct observation of officers and travellers so the redesign addressed the real sequence of actions rather than the documented one.

Trade-off acceptedObservation is slow, it does not scale to every port, and it surfaces variation that is uncomfortable to standardise. It is the only way to avoid designing for a process that nobody actually follows.

Decision 02

Defer collapsing the siloed databases into one analysable store.

WhyA single store would have unlocked analysis the programme genuinely wanted. It also carried the highest risk to port and core-system stability. I sequenced it after the consolidation users depended on, deliberately.

Trade-off acceptedIt cost the programme the analytical capability it most wanted, and left data integration work for a later phase at higher total effort. It protected the operation that cannot go down.

05 — Delivery

Two seaports first, then the rest

  • Two-seaport pilot
  • Seaports live
  • Airports piloting
  • Land ports

Deployment began with a two-seaport pilot rather than a national cutover. The platform is live across seaports today, with airports piloting and land ports to follow.

06 — Outcome

1consolidated platform
4service lines brought together

Consolidation onto one platform with staged rollout, process standardisation across ports that had each evolved their own, and the siloed-database work sequenced for a later phase with the operation intact.

07 — What I'd carry forward

Sequencing is a product decision, and the elegant change is not always the first one. Deferring the single data store cost analytical convenience and bought operational stability — and stating that as a decision, with its cost, is what makes it reviewable rather than an omission.

Where documented process and actual practice have diverged, observation beats specification. Designing from the document would have produced something correct and unusable.
