Ranking framework for government websites

Built with IIT Delhi and IIM Indore, covering 150+ parameters. NeSDA uses it today.

~₹5 croreproject value
150+parameters
The short version

The problem

NeSDA is the national framework that assesses and ranks government websites. The departments it assessed did not report to the project team.

What was done

Used the weight of senior government offices instead of relying on persuasion: Secretary-level communication, CERT-In direction and a named contact in each organisation. The automated scoring was checked against human auditors before anyone relied on it.

The result

Around 100 sites were assessed with 20 auditors, and the scores were tuned until they sat within ±10% of the auditors. India placed among the top five G20 economies in the benchmark, and the framework continues as AKAM (Aanklan).

Senior Consultant · 2022–24

Indian visa system, IVFRT 2.0

Visa, e-visa, OCI and NRI services consolidated onto one national platform.

1platform
Seaportslive
The short version

The problem

Old, separate and slow systems with data-sync problems, serving foreign nationals and tourists at the border, with practice that differed from port to port.

What was done

Designed from watching officers and travellers work, not from the written specification. Kept the separate databases for the first phase, because a port has no maintenance window and merging them carried the most risk.

The result

One consolidated platform in staged rollout: a two-seaport pilot, seaports live, airports piloting and land ports to follow.

Senior Consultant · 2022–24

National Portal of India

One front door to 20+ government sources, in 22 languages.

~₹23 croreproject value
22languages
The short version

The problem

A static directory of disconnected portals. The aim was to bring reliable information from many government sources into one place, in 22 languages.

What was done

Treated translation as something to measure. People reviewed the machine output, accuracy was checked against agreed language criteria, and volunteers did blind surveys. A custom content system brought the different sources together.

The result

About 80% of machine output was accepted as translated, from an early error rate near one sentence in ten. Search and summaries were planned for the next phase.

The short version

The problem

Credentials were issued as NFTs on Quorum for an EY unit. Joining was voluntary, so the product had to earn its own use.

What was done

Gathered requirements from all four user groups before building, and cut the NFT marketplace to protect the core.

Blockchain Architect · 2021–22

Mainframe automation and blockchain

Reconciliation automation for a UK bank, saving about £200,000 a year.

~£200Ka year, client-calculated
The short version

The problem

A manual card-dispute reconciliation on an old mainframe. The real blocker was a permission that had never been set up.

What was done

Got the permission sorted through the client instead of working around it, and rebuilt the pipeline instead of scripting over the manual steps.

The result

About £200,000 a year saved by the client's own calculation, and 40% fewer reported defects at six months. Two blockchain proofs of concept reached production, and the first blockchain lab on TCS's own infrastructure was set up.
