01 · Situation

A national registry, on paper

The Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies, under the Ministry of Cooperation, holds the country's record of multi-state cooperative societies: around 1,800 societies and some 40 million members. The process was largely paper-based.

02 · The constraint

The blocker was the statute, not the software

The easy reading was a technology-modernisation problem. It was not. The MSCS Act recognised neither electronic notice nor a signature that was not physical, so the intended digital workflow had no legal standing however well it was built.

A platform delivered on that foundation would have been a platform nobody could lawfully use. Discovering this during delivery rather than during discovery would have cost the programme.

03 · My mandate

From discovery to ministry-side oversight

I supported the transformation from process discovery and DPR and RFP authorship through to ministry-side delivery oversight, including vendor scope discipline, deviation management and escalation. My role was Senior Consultant.

04 · Decisions

Two calls, each with its cost

Decision 1. Change the constraint, not just the software.

WhyIf the statute does not recognise electronic notice, no amount of product work substitutes. I proposed amendments to the MSCS Act so the intended digital workflow could operate inside a proper legal framework. They were tabled and enacted in about six months.

Trade-off acceptedIt put the programme's critical path through a legislative process nobody on the delivery team controlled. Building something narrower inside the old Act would have shipped sooner, but it was not the workflow that was needed.

Decision 2. Design accountability into the workflow rather than digitising the paper one.

WhyA digitised paper process inherits the paper process's ability to stall silently. The redesigned process introduced role-based access for each officer and automatic escalation at five, seven and fifteen days, so delay became visible by default rather than on request.

Trade-off acceptedAutomatic escalation puts pressure on officers who may be waiting on something outside their control, so it had to be tuned rather than simply switched on. It replaced chasing with a system.

05 · Delivery

Writing the specification, then holding delivery to it

I wrote the DPR and the RFP, then worked on the ministry side of my own specification: vendor scope discipline, deviation management and escalation. That is an unusual and useful position. The person judging whether a deviation is acceptable is the person who wrote what was asked for.

06 · Outcome

A paper office became a live platform

70%+faster application processing

The platform now holds the country's multi-state society record, including the Sahara credit cooperative society data used for refund validation.

07 · What I carry forward

Ask what cannot move

The most valuable thing discovery produces is an honest answer to what actually cannot move. Here it was a statute, and treating it as a requirement rather than a constraint is what made the programme possible.

Writing the specification and then governing against it is worth seeking out. It removes the gap between what was meant and what was asked for.
