GE Vernova Wind Portfolio

01 — Situation

A unit at 100% loss, supporting hardware that does not stop

I inherited an application organisation supporting a wind portfolio at 100% loss, staffed at roughly twice the contractual requirement, under genuine round-the-clock operational obligations. Turbines do not stop at close of business, and neither can the applications watching them.

Scope covered roughly 50 to 55 applications and a team of about 25.

02 — The constraint

The obvious fix was the wrong one

The standard response to a loss-making unit staffed at twice requirement is to cut. That would have removed the cost and left the cause, because the cause was spread across six things at once:

  • Operating model
  • Knowledge management
  • SLA definition
  • Automation
  • Commercial scope
  • Team structure

Critical knowledge lived inside individual senior engineers. Support, enhancements and environment changes were measured as one blended pool, which hid where the service was actually failing. Out-of-scope work was absorbed rather than charged.

03 — My mandate

Own P&L, delivery, operational performance, product responsibilities and people management for the application organisation.

04 — Decisions

Decision 01

Fix the operating model before reducing people.

WhyCutting first removes capacity and keeps the dependency: the work still needs doing and only a few people can do it. I standardised SOPs so knowledge stopped living in individuals, shifted repetitive work left, and established an L1.5 layer — and only then reduced headcount.

Trade-off acceptedIt delayed the visible financial improvement by months while the cost base stayed where it was, which is an uncomfortable thing to hold in front of a P&L review. It made the reduction survivable rather than a transfer of risk onto the people who remained.

Decision 02

Separate the commercial signals.

WhySupport, enhancements and environment changes were being measured as one service pool, so failures in one averaged out against successes in another. Each got its own commitment, which made the actual failure visible and chargeable.

Trade-off acceptedThree commitments are harder to report and to govern than one blended number, and it removed a convenient place to hide. That was the point.

Decision 03

Commercialise scope instead of absorbing it.

WhyOut-of-scope requests were being delivered for free, which hid demand and funded a loss. They became separately scoped work, generating around $200,000 a year of new business.

Trade-off acceptedIt introduced friction into requests that previously just happened, and required holding a line with a client used to getting them. It converted a leak into revenue and made the real demand visible.

Decision 04

Give AI production authority only as fast as the evidence allowed.

WhyAI entered production support in controlled stages — shadow mode, then a six-hour window, then full-day operation — with each expansion resting on evidence from the stage before it. The environment combined rule-based scripting with agentic AI and AIOps on an internal platform.

Trade-off acceptedStaging it meant running human and machine in parallel for longer than strictly necessary, paying twice for the same coverage during shadow. It is why the expansion was never reversed.

05 — Delivery

When the safety alerting failed with technicians on a turbine

A safety-alerting service used by field technicians failed overnight while two technicians were on a turbine. I raised it as P1, activated the on-call rota, and the fault was traced to a third-party regional outage outside the application stack.

Outside our stack did not make it outside our responsibility. Until the provider restored service the team maintained manual all-clear controls and sanitised the affected regions before the incident was closed.

07 — What I'd carry forward

A unit losing money at twice the required headcount looks like a staffing problem and usually is not. Reduce people last: standardise the knowledge, move the work to the right tier, then resize. Reversing that order transfers the risk to whoever is left.

And a blended metric is a place for failure to hide. Separating the commitments cost reporting simplicity and bought the ability to see what was actually wrong.
