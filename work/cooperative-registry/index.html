Context

The situation

The Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies keeps India’s record of multi-state cooperative societies: about 1,800 societies and some 40 million members. Almost everything ran on paper, in a physical office.

Discovery showed the real blocker: the Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act recognised neither electronic notices nor non-physical signatures. A digital platform built on that foundation would have been a platform nobody could lawfully use.

Constraints

What couldn’t move

Statute blocked digital notices and signaturesPaper-only legacy processVendor delivery overseen from the ministry sideSenior-level approvals for each stageRollout without disrupting live registrations
Key decisions

The calls I made, and what each one cost

Change the law before building the software

We proposed amendments so that electronic notices and digital signatures were valid. They were tabled and passed in about six months.

Trade-off acceptedIt put the critical path through a legislative process nobody on the team controlled. A narrower build inside the old Act would have shipped sooner — and not been the process the ministry needed.

Build accountability into the new workflow

A digitised paper process can stall as quietly as the paper one. Each officer got a defined role, and delays escalate automatically at 5, 7 and 15 days.

Trade-off acceptedThe thresholds had to be tuned, because some waits are outside an officer’s control.

Write the specification, then hold the vendor to it

I wrote the DPR and RFP, onboarded the vendor, then oversaw delivery from the ministry side — scope, deviations and escalation.

Trade-off acceptedThe person judging each deviation was the person who wrote what had been asked for. That made calls faster and occasionally harder.

Roll out module by module

Registration went first; other modules followed once each was stable.

Trade-off acceptedA longer overall timeline in exchange for never stopping the registry.

Results

What changed

  • A fully paperless national registry for ~1,800 societies and ~40 million members, still growing.
  • Application processing 70%+ faster — from 15–20 days to about 4.
  • Amendments to the Act passed in about six months, giving digital notices and signatures legal standing.
  • The platform was later extended to validate refund claims for cooperative-society depositors.
Takeaway

What I carry forward

The best outcome of discovery is an honest answer to what cannot move. Treating the law as something to change, not work around, is what made the project possible.
