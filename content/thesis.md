# The thesis — why me

Three pillars. Each is a claim with one piece of hard evidence attached, so the section
argues rather than asserts.

---

## 1. I run the delivery, not just the report

Governance is only worth something if it changes what the team does on Monday. I set up
PMOs, RACI matrices, risk registers, and escalation paths — and then I use them to make
decisions, not to describe decisions that were made somewhere else.

**Evidence:** I took over a 30+ person team on a distressed application portfolio at
Infosys, defined ownership properly, and cut mean time to resolution from four days to
under twelve hours. Attrition and burnout dropped with it. The RACI wasn't the
achievement — what the RACI let us stop arguing about was.

---

## 2. I can argue with the architecture

I spent my first six years writing code — Hyperledger Fabric networks for BFSI clients,
MERN applications, mainframe automation. I still write it. That means when an engineer
tells me something will take six weeks, I can ask why, understand the answer, and tell
the client whether it's true.

**Evidence:** I authored the developer specification for certificate versioning on the
Green Hydrogen certification platform — how an issued certificate gets amended,
superseded, and audited without breaking its traceability chain. That's not a
requirement a project manager writes. It's a design decision, and it needed someone
who understood both the compliance obligation and the data model.

---

## 3. I know how government actually approves things

Most public-sector programmes don't fail in the build. They fail in the approval — the
note that wasn't structured for the person signing it, the costing that didn't match
the rate card, the accessibility standard nobody read until the audit.

**Evidence:** I've authored RFPs, DPRs, and technical proposals through to
Secretary-level approval; costed infrastructure against live NICSI rate cards; and
built to GIGW, UX4G, WCAG 2.1 AA, and CERT-In from the design stage rather than
retrofitting after a failed audit. I've taken a platform from concept note to
production inside that machinery, which is a different skill from building the
platform.

---

## Notes

The three pillars deliberately map to your three resume summary variants — delivery,
technical, public sector. On a resume those compete for the same slot; here they stack
into one argument. That's the main structural gain of a website over a CV, and it's
why I'd resist splitting the site into role-specific versions.
